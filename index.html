Project 1 · AI Response Evaluation

Demonstration project, not a client assignment. This sample illustrates a structured method for evaluating AI-generated responses against explicit criteria.

Evaluation task

Question: What are the most important factors to consider when assessing whether drinking water is safe for household consumption?

Hypothetical Response A

“Safe drinking water should be assessed for microbiological contamination, chemical hazards and physical characteristics. The source, storage conditions and possible contamination during collection and handling should also be considered. Where appropriate, water quality should be checked against applicable health or regulatory standards.”

Hypothetical Response B

“If water looks clear and has no unusual smell, it is safe to drink. Boiling is always enough to remove every possible contaminant.”

Evaluation rubric

CriterionResponse AResponse B
AccuracyStrong: identifies several relevant dimensions without claiming certainty.Weak: visual appearance cannot establish safety, and boiling does not remove every contaminant.
RelevanceStrong: directly addresses household drinking-water safety.Moderate: addresses safety but relies on oversimplification.
CompletenessStrong: covers microbiological, chemical, physical and handling factors.Weak: omits important categories and makes an absolute claim.
SafetyStrong: avoids giving a falsely reassuring conclusion.Weak: could encourage unsafe consumption.
ClarityStrong: concise and understandable.Strong wording, but the underlying claims are misleading.

Overall assessment

Response A is the stronger answer. The key evaluation issue is not simply whether a response contains a true statement. An evaluator should also identify omissions, unsupported absolutes and wording that could create a misleading safety impression.

Skill demonstrated: applying a consistent rubric, identifying factual and safety issues, comparing outputs, and explaining the reasoning behind an evaluation.

Annotation rules

  1. Apply the category that best matches the primary information in the record.
  2. Do not infer missing facts.
  3. Use “Needs review” when the record does not contain enough information for a reliable label.
  4. Apply the same rule consistently across records.

Project 3 · Research & Fact Verification

Demonstration project. A fact-checking workflow should separate the claim from the evidence and avoid treating a partly correct statement as completely correct.

Claim

“Boiling contaminated water makes every type of contaminated water completely safe.”

Assessment

Verdict: Misleading / overbroad. The claim uses an absolute “every type” formulation. A sound evaluation should distinguish between microbial hazards that heat treatment may address and other hazards that boiling does not necessarily remove.

Verification workflow

  1. Identify the exact claim and any absolute language.
  2. Break the claim into testable components.
  3. Check authoritative evidence appropriate to the subject.
  4. Record what the evidence supports and what it does not establish.
  5. Write a conclusion that matches the strength of the evidence.
Skill demonstrated: claim decomposition, evidence-aware reasoning, identification of overstatement and careful reporting.

Project 4 · Environmental Health AI Evaluation

This demonstration applies a domain-oriented evaluation approach to an AI-generated environmental-health answer. It is not presented as clinical or regulatory advice.

Hypothetical AI answer

“Poor sanitation can contribute to disease transmission. Communities can reduce risk through safe sanitation facilities, appropriate waste management, hygiene practices, protection of drinking-water sources and public-health education. Local conditions and applicable guidance should be considered when designing interventions.”

Evaluation

CriterionAssessment
RelevanceStrong. The response addresses sanitation and prevention.
CoverageGood. It identifies several intervention areas without pretending one intervention solves every situation.
SpecificityModerate. A real task might require the evaluator to check whether the intended audience or scenario demands more detail.
SafetyStrong. It avoids an absolute guarantee and recognizes local conditions.
Professional qualityGood. The response is concise and appropriately qualified.
Domain value: environmental-health training and field experience provide context for evaluating whether AI-generated public-health information is appropriately framed, complete and cautious.
